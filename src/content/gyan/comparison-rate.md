Every home loan ad shows two rates: the big one, and a smaller one labelled "comparison rate". The small one is often the more honest of the two.

## Headline rate vs comparison rate

- **The headline (advertised) rate** is the interest rate charged on the loan.
- **The comparison rate** combines that interest rate with most of the upfront and ongoing fees, such as application fees and monthly or annual package fees, into a single percentage.

In Australia, lenders must show a comparison rate alongside the advertised rate. A loan with a low headline rate but high fees can end up with a higher comparison rate than a loan that looks slightly more expensive at first glance.

## The catch: it's based on a standard loan

The comparison rate is calculated on a standard example, a $150,000 loan over 25 years. If your loan is much bigger, which most home loans in Melbourne are, fixed fees matter less in percentage terms, and the comparison rate can overstate their impact.

## What it doesn't include

- Government charges such as stamp duty and mortgage registration fees
- Break costs if you leave a fixed loan early
- Fees that depend on how you use the loan, like late payment fees or some redraw fees
- The value of features such as an offset account

## How to compare properly

1. **Use the comparison rate to shortlist**, not to decide.
2. **Ask for the full fee list** for anything on your shortlist.
3. **Work out the cost for your loan size and plans.** A package fee on a $600,000 loan can be well worth it if it comes with a discount and an offset account you will actually use.
4. **Think about how long you'll keep the loan.** Upfront fees hurt more if you refinance after a year.

The cheapest-looking loan isn't always the cheapest loan. The comparison rate is a good starting point for working out which one is.
