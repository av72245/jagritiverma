Applying with five banks to see who says yes feels like smart shopping. To a lender looking at your credit file, it can look like five banks said no.

## What a credit enquiry is

When you formally apply for credit, whether a home loan, credit card, car loan or buy now pay later account, the lender usually checks your credit report. That check is recorded on your file as an enquiry, showing who looked, when and for what kind of credit.

Enquiries can stay on your credit report for years, and they can affect your credit score.

## Why lots of enquiries look risky

Lenders can't always see why you applied several times. A cluster of recent applications can suggest:

- you are under financial pressure,
- you were declined elsewhere, or
- you are about to take on a lot of new debt at once.

None of that may be true, but it can lead to a lower score, extra questions, or a decline.

## Shopping around the smarter way

1. **Compare before you apply.** Look at rates, fees and features first. You don't need to apply to see a lender's products.
2. **Use a broker.** A broker can compare many lenders and check your situation against their rules before an application goes in, so you apply once, where it is most likely to succeed.
3. **Check your own credit report.** You can get your report free from the Australian credit reporting bodies. Checking your own file doesn't harm your score.
4. **Space out other applications.** Hold off on new cards or car finance in the months before a home loan.

## Your file isn't only enquiries

Australia uses comprehensive credit reporting, so your file also shows how you repay your debts. A steady record of on-time repayments helps your score over time.

Fewer, better-targeted applications protect your credit file. Talk it through first, then apply where it makes sense.
