Pre-approval (lenders also call it conditional approval or approval in principle) is one of the most misunderstood steps in buying a home. It feels like a yes. It is really a "yes, if".

## What pre-approval actually is

A lender looks at your income, spending, debts and credit history and says: based on what we can see today, we would likely lend you up to this amount. It gives you a realistic budget and shows agents and vendors you are a serious buyer.

What it has not done yet is look at the property. The lender has not valued it, checked the contract or confirmed nothing has changed in your situation.

## Why it can still fall through

- **The valuation comes in low.** If the lender values the home below the price you agreed to pay, they lend against the lower figure, and you need to cover the gap.
- **Your situation changes.** A new job, a new car loan, a fresh credit card or a drop in income between pre-approval and settlement can all change the answer.
- **The property doesn't fit the lender's rules.** Some lenders are cautious about certain apartments, rural properties or small units.
- **It expires.** Pre-approvals usually last around three to six months, depending on the lender. After that, your details need to be checked again.

## How to use it well

1. **Get it before you start bidding**, especially if you plan to buy at auction, where there is no cooling-off period.
2. **Keep your finances steady** until settlement. Hold off on new debts, big purchases and job changes where you can.
3. **Don't max it out.** The pre-approved amount is a ceiling, not a target. Leave room for costs and for life.
4. **Ask what conditions are attached.** Some pre-approvals depend on things like a savings history or a particular deposit amount.

## The step after pre-approval

Once you have a signed contract, the lender assesses the property and issues formal, or unconditional, approval. That is the point where the loan is properly locked in, subject to final settlement steps.

So pre-approval is a green light to start looking with confidence. It just isn't the finish line.
