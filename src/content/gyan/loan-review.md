Most people spend weeks choosing a home loan, then don't look at it again for years. Meanwhile rates move, new products launch, and lenders often save their sharpest rates for new customers.

## Why a yearly check makes sense

- **The rate gap.** Existing customers can end up paying more than new customers at the same lender for the same type of loan.
- **Your life changes.** A pay rise, a new baby, a business, an investment property or a plan to renovate can change which loan suits you.
- **Your property value changes.** If your home has grown in value, you may have more equity. That can mean access to better rates, or removing the need for lenders mortgage insurance on a new loan.

## Signs it's time to review now

- Your fixed rate is ending soon
- Your repayments feel tight
- You're paying fees for features you don't use, or missing features you need, like an offset
- It's been more than a year since you compared

## Step one: just ask

Sometimes a phone call to your current lender asking for a better rate is enough. Have a competing offer in mind before you call.

## If you're thinking of refinancing

Switching lenders can save money, but compare the full picture:

- Discharge fees from your current lender
- Application and valuation fees with the new lender
- **Break costs** if you're leaving a fixed rate early, which can be large
- Whether the new loan term resets, and how that affects total interest
- Any cashback offers, and what conditions come with them

A lower rate only helps if the savings outweigh the switching costs over the time you'll keep the loan.

## Make it a habit

Pick a date each year, such as your loan anniversary or tax time, and do a five-minute check. Small rate differences add up to real money over the life of a loan.
