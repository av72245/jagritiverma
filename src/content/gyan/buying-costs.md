Saving a deposit is the big milestone. But when you get to settlement, the bill is usually bigger than the deposit alone. Here's what else to plan for.

## Stamp duty

Called land transfer duty in Victoria, this is usually the largest extra cost. It's a state government tax based on the property's price, and the rules and rates differ from state to state.

Many states offer exemptions or concessions for eligible first home buyers, sometimes up to a price cap. Check your state revenue office's calculator for the current figures before you set a budget.

## Government fees

- **Mortgage registration fee:** registers the lender's interest in the property.
- **Transfer fee:** records you as the new owner.

These are smaller amounts but easy to forget.

## Conveyancing or legal fees

A conveyancer or solicitor reviews the contract, handles searches and manages settlement. Fees vary, so get a quote upfront and ask what's included.

## Inspections

A building and pest inspection before you commit can save you from expensive surprises. For apartments and units, a strata or owners corporation report shows the building's finances and any planned works.

## Loan costs

- Application or establishment fees, if your lender charges them
- **Lenders mortgage insurance (LMI)** if your deposit is under 20%, which can be added to the loan or paid upfront
- Valuation fees with some lenders

## The move itself

Removalists, connecting utilities and internet, and insurance from settlement day onwards. Building insurance is often required by the lender from settlement.

## Build a buffer

Once you add it all up, set aside a little extra on top. Keeping some savings after settlement gives you breathing room for the first repayments and anything unexpected in the new home.

Knowing the full number early means no last-minute scramble, and no buying above what feels comfortable.
