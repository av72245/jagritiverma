An offset account is an everyday bank account that quietly works against your home loan. It's one of the simplest ways to pay less interest without locking your money away.

## How it works

The balance in your offset account is subtracted from your loan balance when the lender works out interest. Interest on home loans is usually calculated daily, so every dollar sitting in the offset helps every day it's there.

**A simple example:** you owe $500,000 and have $30,000 in a full offset account. You pay interest as if you owed $470,000. Your repayment usually stays the same, so more of it goes to paying down the loan itself, and the loan can be paid off sooner.

## Full vs partial offset

Most offsets are **full (100%)**, where every dollar counts. Some loans offer **partial** offsets, where only a percentage of the balance counts. Check which one you're getting.

## Offset vs redraw

Both reduce your interest, but they're not the same:

- **Offset:** your money stays in a separate account. You can spend it, get your salary paid into it and use a card on it.
- **Redraw:** you've paid extra into the loan itself. You can usually take it back out, but the lender may set limits or fees, and the money is technically part of the loan.

For investors especially, the difference can matter for tax, so get advice from a tax professional before choosing.

## Is it worth it?

Loans with offset accounts sometimes have a slightly higher rate or a package fee. An offset pays off when you'll keep a meaningful balance in it, such as savings, an emergency fund or your salary passing through. If your balance will usually be close to zero, a cheaper basic loan may suit you better.

## Getting the most from it

- Have your salary paid straight into the offset
- Keep savings and emergency funds there rather than in a separate savings account
- Pay everyday expenses with a credit card you clear in full each month, if that works for you, so money stays in the offset longer

Same money, working harder.
